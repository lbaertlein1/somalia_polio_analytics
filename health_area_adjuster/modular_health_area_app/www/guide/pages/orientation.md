## Purpose

This page's main job isn't the landmarks themselves — it's two things at once, before you start health area planning:

- **Orienting you to the district** — its extent, its layout, and how to actually find places on it. Dropping pins on places you recognize is how you confirm you can read the map correctly.
- **Building familiarity with the mapping mechanics** you'll use everywhere else in the tool — switching base layers, zooming and panning, clicking to add a point, dragging to reposition it, and renaming it in a table. Every one of those actions reappears on later tabs (painting boundaries, moving facility pins, renaming team areas), so this is a low-stakes place to get comfortable with them first.

Landmarks themselves don't influence any boundary or calculation anywhere in the tool. Their only other effect is showing up as a reference point on later maps (health area mapping, team area mapping, and elsewhere), so you and others have a familiar point of reference while painting boundaries.

## What counts as a landmark

Any recognizable reference point: a town, village, school, mosque, road junction, or river crossing — anything that helps orient someone looking at the map.

## Adding, renaming, and moving a landmark

- **Add**: click anywhere on the map to drop a pin.
- **Reposition**: drag the pin to move it.
- **Rename**: edit the Name column directly in the table on the right.
- **Locate**: click a row in the table to pan the map to that landmark.
- **Remove one**: click the **×** in that row's Delete column, or use **Clear all landmarks** to remove everything at once.

Take the time this page invites — zoom in and out, pan around, and try switching base layers (the stacked-squares control, top right of the map) to find the view that works best for you before moving on.

## Reviewing contextual data

The map also shows several reference layers that appear again throughout the tool — this is a good place to get familiar with what each one means, since none of them are things you edit here:

- **IDP Settlement** (red dot) — a displaced-persons settlement location, sourced externally (see [Admin](admin) for the data source URL). These matter for planning since IDP settlements can shift a district's population distribution, and campaign coverage figures downstream account for them.
- **Subdivision** (purple dashed line) — an existing administrative subdivision boundary, where available. Shown for geographic reference only — it doesn't have to match, constrain, or relate to health area or team area boundaries.
- **Settlement Extent** (teal dashed line) — the WHO-defined built-up/urban area boundary, where available. Also reference-only, showing roughly where a settlement's developed area actually is versus its surrounding open land.

## Submitting

**Submit Landmarks** saves them to the database. **Continue →** moves you forward in the workflow — normally to [Campaign Scope](campaign-scope) or straight to [Facilities](facilities), depending on whether an admin has marked this district as full or partial coverage (see [Admin](admin) for where that's decided — it isn't decided here).
